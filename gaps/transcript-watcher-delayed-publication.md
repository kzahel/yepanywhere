# Known transcript appends can take seconds to reach the catalog

After cold Inbox discovery, the first event for a known Claude append reached YA's watcher entry point 0.30–9.3 seconds after the write in eight early mixed-profile runs. The shortest and longest samples came from sequential runs. Four windows with no event in 12 seconds came from a batch with overlapping replay instances. Concurrent fixture generation could contribute to those longer delays. Delivering a diagnostic hint for the same write allowed the catalog to catch up. Trace the active observation source (directory watch, fixed-file watch, poll or rescan) to explain the delay. The existing measurement starts where those sources converge. A final sequential pair needed no rescue and reproduced a 7.69-second first-event delay even with zero full catalog reads.

Continuous output also exposes a separate delay in the per-file trailing debounce. With one synthetic immediate hint for each of 60 real writes spaced 100 ms apart, YA kept restarting its 200 ms debounce and emitted one direct change after the stream ended. Natural delivery exposed 0–8 of 59 intermediate versions across the original large ordering runs and final sequential pair. The real provider write cadence matters here. Investigate a bounded publication interval for a busy path while preserving final exact versions, coalescing and idle resource use. These freshness issues remain after the CPU ordering control eliminates the second startup inventory.
